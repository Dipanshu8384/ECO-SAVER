10 Easy Everyday Eco-Friendly Habits

Small, consistent actions can make a big difference for the environment.

  • Carry a reusable bag and water bottle.
  • Switch off lights/electronics when not in use.
  • Walk, cycle, or use public transport.
  • Refuse single-use plastics and avoid packaged foods.
  • Segregate waste and manage food portions to cut waste.

Energy-Efficient Home Guide

Make your house greener and save money:

  • Replace old bulbs with LEDs.
  • Insulate doors/windows and use thick curtains.
  • Choose BEE star-rated appliances.
  • Use solar panels or water heaters if possible.
  • Run machines with full loads and air dry laundry.

Beginner’s Guide to Recycling

Start your recycling journey with the basics:

  • Separate wet (organic) and dry (recyclable) waste at home
  • Clean recyclables (like bottles, cans) before binning
  • Follow your city’s collection system—know your pickup days!
  • Avoid recycling items with food residue or mixed materials

What Can Be Recycled?

Learn which materials are recyclable and how to identify them:

  • Paper, cardboard, clean plastics (types #1, #2, #5), glass, and metals are widely accepted
  • Check for the recycling symbol on packaging
  • Avoid recycling soft plastics, used tissues, or soiled containers
  • Some e-waste and batteries have special drop-off locations

Composting at Home: Why & How

Turn kitchen waste into useful compost for plants and gardens:

  • Collect fruit/veggie peels, tea leaves, coffee grounds in a bin
  • Alternate dry (leaves, paper) and wet (food waste) layers
  • Keep moist and turn regularly for faster results
  • Reduces landfill waste and creates nutrient-rich soil

Wet Waste vs Dry Waste

Know the difference for effective segregation:

  • Wet: Food scraps, peels, leftovers (compostable)
  • Dry: Paper, plastics, metals, glass (recyclable)
  • Segregation leads to more recycling and less landfill
  • Hazardous: Medicines, cleaners, batteries; handle separately
